## TSMC Employee Data Breach Linked to Cloud Provider

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) has confirmed that personal employee data has been leaked online after a threat actor put **6.9GB** of information allegedly stolen from the company up for auction. The technology giant stated that the incident affected current and former employees but does not impact its operations or customers. 

> "TSMC has found that some of our current and former employee data was made available on an external website without authorization," TSMC says in a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). The company added, "Currently, we have found no evidence that TSMC's systems have been compromised."

TSMC's investigation revealed that the leaked information matched data stored in a cloud service provider it uses, indicating a breach outside its own infrastructure. The company is now notifying impacted staff and providing the necessary support, while also engaging a third-party cybersecurity firm to help manage the situation.

On **November 8**, someone using the alias **'SmithBianl'** leaked on a hacker forum a text file containing **499 rows** of information allegedly stolen from TSMC. The actor claimed the file was a sample of a **6.9GB** dataset containing **46,000** employee records. According to the post, the leaked information included TSMC employees' national ID numbers, addresses, contact details, and banking information. SmithBianl offered the data for auction, with bidding starting at **3 BTC** (approximately **$65,000**). In the absence of a buyer, they warned that the data would be leaked for free in one week, setting the first deadline to **November 15**.
