## Online Scam Volumes Double to Overtake Phishing 🚀

Detections of online scams have doubled between July 2025 and June 2026, as cybercriminals focus on more direct ways to generate revenue from victims. Akamai's latest State of the Internet (SOTI) report, featuring data from partner Bolster AI, revealed that scam activity has overtaken phishing for the first time. The report recorded around **6.4 million online scams** in the period of April to June 2026, compared to **3.2 million** in July to September 2025. This category typically includes fake stores, investment scams, tech support scams, and payment fraud.

> "The fight against fraud is no longer just an isolated financial function; it is a critical, cross-functional cybersecurity imperative," said **Patrick Sullivan**, CTO of security strategy at Akamai. He added, "As autonomous AI agents and synthetic identities redefine how customers interact with brands, threat actors are exploiting these same vectors to conduct business logic abuse and zero-click data exfiltration."

However, phishing—which Akamai defines as attempts to steal credentials, authentication info, and data via brand impersonation—also rose during this period. Phishing detections increased by **61%**, driven by reusable phishing kits, industrialized infrastructure, and AI. AI has helped to decrease the average phishing attack lifespan by more than **50%**, dropping from **48 days** (Q4 2025) to **22 days** (Q2 2026).

> "Adversaries leverage AI to generate dynamic templates, produce multilingual lures at scale, and deploy condition-aware evasion—drastically lowering costs while bypassing legacy defenses," Akamai explained. "Conversely, security teams use AI to compress investigation and reduce workflows from days to less than an hour through automated indicator analysis, infrastructure clustering, and evidence collection, disrupting attacks before victims are impacted."

Over **80%** of scam victims in the US reported suicidal thoughts following the crime, according to new data from the non-profit **Identity Theft Resource Center (ITRC)** published on October 28. "When general consumer scam victims overcame their shame to seek help, institutions frequently compounded the injury," the ITRC explained. "Nearly **nine in 10** general consumer scam victims asked an organization for help and were never contacted, and **67.2%** reported that the organizations they contacted 'made me feel it was my own fault.'"

A lack of support can also leave victims exposed to recovery scams. "Abandoned by legitimate institutions and desperate to undo the damage, **78.9%** of general consumer scam victims (and **90.9%** of those experiencing suicidal ideation) paid a fee to a third party who promised to recover their stolen money, fix their credit, or catch the scammer—compared to just **6.5%** of scam victims who connected with the ITRC," the report stated.
