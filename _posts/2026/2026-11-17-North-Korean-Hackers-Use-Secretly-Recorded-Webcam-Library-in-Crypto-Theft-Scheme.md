## North Korean Hackers Use Secretly Recorded Webcam Library in Crypto Theft Scheme 🚨

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has issued a FLASH alert to the cryptocurrency and web3 industry regarding a highly effective social engineering scheme known as **"FaceStealer"**. This operation is attributed to North Korean state-sponsored hackers, including groups like Sapphire Sleet, UNC1069, Dangerous Password, CryptoCore, APT38, BlueNoroff, and Stardust Chollima. Over the past 18 months, these hackers have stolen more than **$400 million** in cryptocurrency and attempted to compromise the open-source software supply chain.

### How the FaceStealer Scheme Works

FaceStealer employs imposter versions of popular video conferencing tools such as Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meets, which are controlled by the hackers. Targets are lured into fake meetings through invitations sent from hijacked accounts of previous victims. Once a target joins a fake meeting, hackers play **prerecorded webcam videos** of past victims to simulate a legitimate video call. They then introduce a mock technical error and impersonate the victim's trusted contact, providing instructions that lead to malware installation on the target's computer. 

### Consequences of Compromise

Once the victim's computer is compromised, the North Korean hackers can:
- Steal accessible cryptocurrency
- Exploit the victim's access to external infrastructure, such as an employer's network
- Access the victim's contact lists and chat histories

The FBI has identified and seized over **900 victim webcam videos** used to convincingly impersonate victims in follow-on attacks. The scheme begins with an already compromised victim, and in successful cases, the hackers have:
1. Scanned the victim's device for cryptocurrency wallets and seed phrases.
2. Taken over the victim's Telegram account.
3. Recorded the victim while waiting for a video chat to start.

### The Role of Typo-Squatted Domains

Shortly before a scheduled meeting, the hacker uses the victim's ECA to send a link that appears to be a legitimate Zoom or Teams meeting link. However, these links are actually typo-squatted domains controlled by the hackers, such as uu01webzoom.us and teams.livemeet.us. Following these instructions downloads malware that installs browser cookie harvesters, keyloggers, screen capturers, and backdoors onto the infected device.

### The Use of Super Intelligence

The hackers behind the FaceStealer campaign are now utilizing **super intelligence (SI)** to enhance the efficiency of their operations, including the generation of deepfake videos.
