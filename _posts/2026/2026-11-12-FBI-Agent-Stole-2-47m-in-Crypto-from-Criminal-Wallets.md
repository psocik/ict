## FBI Agent Stole $2.47m in Crypto from Criminal Wallets 🚨

Patrick Steven Yaroch pleaded guilty on November 5 to wire fraud and money laundering after stealing approximately **$2.47 million** from cryptocurrency wallets linked to FBI investigations. He stole the money from wallets held by individuals the FBI was investigating, including adversarial nation-state actors. Starting no later than December 2024, he made around **100 transfers** using credentials tied to **35 separate investigations**. According to the Department of Justice (DOJ), his actions compromised multiple investigations.

The FBI's search affidavit states that Yaroch accessed FBI systems containing wallet seed phrases and memorized them. A seed phrase is a series of words, commonly **12 or 24**, used to restore access to a cryptocurrency wallet. Anyone with the seed phrase can potentially control the funds. He then used the memorized phrases to transfer funds to a personal wallet on his own device. He subsequently swapped assets into different currencies and made further transactions to try and hide their origins.

Yaroch justified his initial actions by expressing frustration that the FBI wasn't acting quickly enough against the wallets' criminal owners. He decided to "take matters into his own hands," according to the affidavit. However, the DOJ revealed that he also used some of the proceeds to cover personal debts, including credit cards, car payments, and other loans. The affidavit also disclosed that Yaroch had asked **ChatGPT** how to invest a million dollars, along with advice on where to live in Europe. It suggested **Portugal**, and he began the process of setting up a life there. According to the affidavit, he approached a former DOJ colleague to confess because his actions were "eating him up inside." The employee urged him to self-report, which he did.

The FBI placed him on leave and then terminated his employment two days later. They showed up at his home with a warrant and seized assets, including his phone, which had access to a **Kraken cryptocurrency account**. They also seized a piece of paper containing the seed phrases for his own account. This isn't an isolated incident. In December 2024, former Secret Service agent **Anthony Joseph Evans** pleaded guilty to stealing cryptocurrency worth **$56,000** from a hardware wallet held in the agency's secure evidence vault.
