## Bulgaria Discovers Massive Government Breach 🚨

Bulgaria has uncovered a **five-year cyberespionage campaign** that stole **130GB** of government files, which ran undetected since **2021**. The Ministry for Innovation and Digital Transformation announced it had uncovered this hacking operation. According to **Reuters**, more than **130 GB** of documents, approximately **62 million pages**, were downloaded from government databases over those five years. Minister **Ivan Vassilev** stated that there is currently no evidence that Bulgarian citizens' personal data was compromised.

> "The minister, Ivan Vassilev, said that since 2021 more than 130 GB, or 62 million pages, of documents had been downloaded, but that there was no evidence so far that Bulgarians' personal data had been compromised," **Nova TV** reported on its website.

The breach came to light only after Bulgaria introduced a new national cybersecurity monitoring system, which detected suspicious activity in **August**. Until then, the country apparently had no system capable of detecting an intrusion that had been ongoing since **2021**. Several international hacking groups may be behind the attack, but investigators have not linked it to any specific country or nation-state actor. Vassilev stated, "We discovered that several international hacker groups had accessed Bulgarian systems starting in **2021**." He added, "The information they stole included sensitive details of how Bulgarian state institutions operate."

After the attack was discovered, several agencies joined the response, including intelligence services, the defense ministry, police, cybersecurity experts, and international partners such as the **EU's cybersecurity agency**. Authorities have isolated the affected systems, blocked the attackers' access, and removed the malware. Prosecutors have also been informed, and Vassilev wants them to investigate not only the hackers but also whether government agencies failed to detect the intrusion earlier. An attack that went unnoticed from **2021** to **2026** raises questions about the attackers' skills and highlights the weaknesses in security monitoring that allowed them to remain hidden for so long.

This isn't the first time Bulgaria has faced a major breach. In **2019**, hackers stole roughly **11 GB** of data from the National Revenue Agency, exposing personal details of nearly **five million citizens**. This time, the target was institutional correspondence rather than citizen records, and the scale of documents taken, **130 GB** compared to **11 GB**, dwarfs that earlier case. What's missing from Wednesday's announcement is almost everything that would let the public judge the real damage: who did this, what specific documents went out the door, and whether any of it has already been used. Bulgaria's own cybersecurity agency, **BTA**, described the find as "Government Uncovers Apparent Years-Long Breach of Administrative Networks," indicating that the investigation is still very much open.
