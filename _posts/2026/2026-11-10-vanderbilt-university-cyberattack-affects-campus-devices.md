## Vanderbilt University Cyberattack Affects Campus Devices 🚨

Vanderbilt University is currently investigating an active cyberattack that may impact university devices, systems, and cloud services. The university has advised students and employees to shut down their devices, disconnect from Vanderbilt Wi-Fi, and contact IT support if they notice any suspicious activity.

The **BYOD ransomware gang** has claimed responsibility for this attack, stating that they have encrypted databases and stolen sensitive data, including Social Security numbers of students and employees. This group has been targeting universities, healthcare centers, and companies since late September.

### What to Do? 🤔

All members of the Vanderbilt community are urged to:
- Shut down university devices
- Disconnect from Vanderbilt's Wi-Fi
- Remain extremely vigilant for signs of compromise, such as unexpected pop-ups, unusual changes to files, or unfamiliar applications.

Students and employees should not attempt to troubleshoot any issues themselves; instead, they should contact IT support immediately.

### The Threat 📉

A few hours after the attack, the BYOD gang took credit, claiming they have locked the databases and possess over **1 Million** Social Security Numbers, full names, email addresses, phone numbers, and student and employee records. They are threatening to release this stolen data unless negotiations occur.

There is also a YouTube video circulating that shows how infected university devices are playing a warning message indicating that the network has been breached by BYOD.

This gang is relatively new, having only become active in late September, and has already caused significant disruptions, including a previous attack on the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center that forced a top-tier hospital to turn away ambulances.
