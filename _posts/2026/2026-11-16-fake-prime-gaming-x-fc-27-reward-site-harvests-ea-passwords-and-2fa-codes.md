## 🚨 Fake Prime Gaming x FC 27 Reward Site Alert!

A fake **"FC27 x Prime Gaming"** reward site has emerged, leading to a copied EA sign-in form that collects passwords and 2FA codes, putting gamers' EA accounts at serious risk! 🎮💔 

Following the game's launch on **September 25**, we identified this deceptive page targeting players' login details. Prime Gaming, which offers games and in-game rewards as part of an Amazon Prime membership, has been exploited by scammers. The fraudulent page mimics Prime Gaming's branding, misleading visitors into believing their rewards are waiting and prompting them to **"connect"** their EA accounts.

### What Happens on the Fake Site?
On this fraudulent site, there are **no rewards**! The **"EA sign-in"** that follows is a copy hosted on the scammers' own domain. It requests your email address, password, and the security code from your two-factor authentication (2FA), sending each piece of information to a server controlled by the criminals. The sign-in page closely imitates EA's own, and the social sign-in buttons are non-functional. 

If your account has 2FA enabled, a **"Verify your identity"** page will prompt you to click **"Send Code"** and enter the six-digit code, which goes straight to the attackers. Capturing a valid 2FA code could allow them to complete a sign-in before it expires, bypassing that security check for a specific login. Victims are then redirected to EA's legitimate Ultimate Team web app at **ea.com**.

### Why This Matters
An EA account is more than just a login; it can hold an FC Ultimate Team club with valuable coins and tradable players, access to purchased games, and saved payment methods. Losing access could jeopardize years of purchases and progress! ⏳💸 

### What to Do If You’ve Been Affected
If you entered your login details on a page like this, go directly to **ea.com** and change your password immediately, even if you didn't submit a 2FA code. Review your security settings and trusted devices, remove any unfamiliar devices, and check for changes to your email address, linked accounts, and purchases. Keep 2FA enabled and change any reused passwords elsewhere, especially on your email account. 

To stay safe, always start at EA's website. Type **ea.com** into your browser and navigate from there. Before entering your EA password or security code, ensure the sign-in page is on **ea.com** or a genuine subdomain, such as **accounts.ea.com**.
