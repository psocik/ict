## Cybercrime: A Global Crisis 🌍

New figures have revealed that cybercrime victims lose an average of **$9,468** in each incident, highlighting the scale of this growing global issue. According to a report from Comparitech, **130.9 million people** are affected each year, leading to an annual global toll of just over **$1.24 trillion**. A previous study from December 2023 indicated that the average loss per victim was **$8,069**, affecting **88.5 million people** and resulting in total losses of **$714 billion**. This trend shows a worrying mix of illegal activities that continue to escalate.

The **United States** remains a prime target for cybercriminals, with **6.7 million victims** suffering losses of **$138.9 billion**, averaging around **$20,731** per victim—more than double the global average. In contrast, countries like **Spain, France, Sweden,** and **Turkey** report average losses of about **$10,000** per victim. Interestingly, **India** has the highest number of scam victims worldwide, with **18.8 million** victims, although its average loss per victim is significantly lower at around **$835**.

Moreover, **China**, with **1.2 million victims**, has reported losses of approximately **$11.5 billion**, aligning with global estimates by Comparitech.

Comparitech's figure of **$1.24 trillion** is considered conservative, as it only accounts for victim losses. Experts predict that the global cost could reach **$10.5 trillion** by 2025, describing this as the greatest transfer of economic wealth in history. Comparitech's findings provide a sobering reality: if these estimates are compared to the GDP of entire countries, the impact of cybercrime would rank **20th** in the world.
