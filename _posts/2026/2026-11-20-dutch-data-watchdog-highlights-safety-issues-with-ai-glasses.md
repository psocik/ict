## Dutch Data Watchdog Highlights Safety Issues with AI Glasses

The Dutch Data Protection Authority (AP) has officially advised consumers against buying AI-powered smart glasses after safety tests revealed concerning behaviors beyond their controversial recording capabilities. The AP recently tested six different models of smart glasses and found glaring issues, including a willingness to display dating advice to an adult glasses-wearer who expressed an interest in a minor. In another case involving a cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) simulation, testers noted that one pair of glasses informed the wearer that emergency services had been called and were scheduled to arrive at the scene, despite no one having made the call. 

Some glasses did not properly process safety warnings on children's toys, others made mistakes when reading medication leaflets, and some misinterpreted warnings for extreme weather events. 

**"Glasses that film you unnoticed are already concerning. Glasses that also give you incorrect advice when someone's life is at stake go a step too far,"** said Monique Verdier, vice chair at the AP. She added, **"AI offers many opportunities, but we must be able to trust that technology. With the camera glasses examined, that trust cannot currently be justified."** 

The AP doubled down on these earlier criticisms, saying the latest tests showed that the AI functions rely on processing data using servers mostly located outside of the European Union. Relatedly, it found that users were not given sufficient information about how the AI was processing the data they captured. 

On a cybersecurity note, the tests threw up a range of issues, including the ability for people nearby the glasses-wearer to connect to the device without permission, view recordings, and in some cases operate the camera. Its tests found that with **"virtually all of the camera glasses examined,"** the recording lights that are supposed to clearly show when a user is recording were dim and easily coverable. The AP said that models could also be wangled into recording even when that light indicator was covered, which goes against what manufacturers so often claim. **"As a result, bystanders can barely see whether they are being filmed,"** it said. 

The watchdog's official stance on the technology, as it is today, deems smart glasses **"unsafe... due to the risks to users and bystanders."** There are also worries about how easily the devices can get past security checks, potentially providing opportunities to those who wish to cause greater harm than a mere blight on personal privacy. A joint investigation by The Observer and BBC Radio 4 recently revealed how AI-enabled glasses went undetected through the airport-style security scanners used in the UK parliament. The two news outlets found that a £60 ($78) pair of Chinese-made glasses bypassed the scanners and could be used to read upside-down documents on MPs' desks, recording data such as phone numbers and addresses to be accessed at a later point.
