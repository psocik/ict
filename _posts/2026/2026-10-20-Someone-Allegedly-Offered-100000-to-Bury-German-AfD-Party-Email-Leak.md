## Major Cyber Breach Exposed!

A hacker claims to have infiltrated Germany's far-right **Alternative für Deutschland (AfD)** party, extracting hundreds of thousands of emails. Now, the alleged attacker says someone has offered **€100,000** to make the leak disappear! 💰

The hacker claims to have exfiltrated more than **421,000 internal emails** and over **100,000 files**. The data was allegedly extracted from more than **800 hosts** associated with the party's domain. It is distributed on the attackers' website via a torrent file weighing nearly **300GB**. The alleged haul includes the mailbox of **Alexander Gauland**, the AfD's former co-leader, as well as internal databases and other party documents.

The party has confirmed a cyber incident to the German newspaper **Bild**. "In September 2026, there was a hacking attack on a hosting provider used by our party," an AfD spokesperson said. The party did not provide any information on the extent of the data theft. The spokesperson claimed that the attack was quickly stopped and all affected users have been informed. The authenticity of the leaked emails remains unverified.

The website shows **AI-generated email summaries**, sorted into categories such as **racism** and **antisemitism**, and graded from **Low to Critical**. According to Cybernews researchers who examined the website, the sender and recipient emails, timestamps, and subjects are shown. The newest entry dates to **September 12th** this year, and the oldest goes back to **2013**. The leaked emails reportedly contain a disturbing collection of references to **Nazism**, **antisemitism**, **racism**, and **political violence** involving AfD members and staff.

Among the reported examples is a **2018 email** forwarded by Alexander Gauland concerning a "Hitler-Wallfahrt," or Hitler pilgrimage. In another instance, a party member reportedly joked about turning refugees into handbags and boots, an apparent reference to Nazi concentration camp atrocities. Other messages allegedly reveal a more openly aggressive political culture, including a Bavarian official reportedly urging party leaders to chant "Merkel verrecke," or "Merkel, croak."

The hacker behind the breach reportedly told **International Cyber Digest** that the operation began with a single unpatched website belonging to the AfD. The objective was to expose what they described as the party's "degeneracy and hypocrisy." "The amount of financial misuse in these documents is appalling, regardless of what you think of immigrants," the hacker told International Cyber Digest. "Hacktivism is often the only answer to populism of this magnitude." 

The story took another turn when, according to International Cyber Digest, an anonymous user contacted the hacker through **Proton Mail** and offered money in exchange for taking the leak site offline. "How can we get in touch? We would pay you money to take the site offline," the message reportedly read. The hacker told the publication that the offer amounted to **€100,000**. However, the hacker intends to keep the website online.
