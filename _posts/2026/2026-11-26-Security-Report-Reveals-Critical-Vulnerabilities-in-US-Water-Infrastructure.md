## Security Report Reveals Critical Vulnerabilities in US Water Infrastructure

Water infrastructure in the United States faces significant security risks as a new study highlights widespread vulnerabilities across technology vendors. A report from exposure management firm Securin reveals that 236 technology vendors providing equipment to US water systems contain nearly twice as many vulnerabilities as identified in public federal records. Crucially, while the federal government has cataloged 1,792 exploited vulnerabilities, researchers found 20 additional flaws currently being exploited that remain absent from the official Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. The analysis indicates that security advisories for water technology hit their highest level since 2020.

However, the report emphasizes that software bugs are not the only threat facing these vital systems. Many real-world attacks, for example, those linked to Iranian actors targeting over 100 water systems across 12 states, exploit poorly secured, internet-connected controllers. Attackers frequently bypass traditional defenses by targeting cellular modems and using default passwords.

Regarding critical infrastructure components, 791 Rockwell MicroLogix 1400 controllers were identified as exposed to the internet as of November 17. Experts warn that utilities must prioritize identifying undocumented cellular modems installed by third-party contractors. Implementing multifactor authentication and removing these devices from the open internet are considered essential steps to prevent unauthorized access to critical infrastructure.
