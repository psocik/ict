## US Intelligence Agencies Sustain Breach of Unclassified Information

The New York Times reports that the **US Central Intelligence Agency**, the **National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency**, and the **Office of the Director of National Intelligence** sustained a breach of an open-source intelligence analysis system, exposing some unclassified information. The hackers reportedly used the open-source, AI-driven pentesting tool **ARTEX** to conduct the attack. The FBI issued an advisory on ARTEX earlier this month. The Office of the Director of National Intelligence stated that there were no disruptions to critical capabilities. A source told the Times that the incident has been contained. 🚨

Additionally, **Microsoft** has declared a "Code Red" effort to improve how it prevents and responds to outages, following a global outage that brought down **Microsoft 365** on November 12th and 13th. M365 Core President **Jason Henderson** said in a memo to staff that the incident was the most customer-impacting incident in the history of **Exchange Online**, and customers had never been without email for so long. Henderson stated, "Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team." 🔴

Researchers at **GreyNoise** warn that an ongoing attack campaign exploited vulnerabilities to breach **Check Point Security** management servers at 22 organizations. Check Point has confirmed that the hackers targeted vulnerabilities that received patches in July 2026. Check Point told **BleepingComputer** in a statement, "The attackers used **CVE-2026-16232**, a SmartConsole authentication bypass vulnerability that Check Point publicly disclosed in July 2026, with a note that exploitation had been observed in the wild. This vulnerability and available protections are therefore not new." The GreyNoise article also reports that the attackers used an adaptation of the publicly available **Metasploit** module for CVE-2026-16232, so the exploitation method described is also previously known. 🔍

In other news, **CrowdStrike** has agreed to acquire zero-trust security firm **Zscaler** in an all-stock transaction valued at approximately **$43.9 billion**, in what Reuters notes will be the largest cybersecurity acquisition of all time. 💰
