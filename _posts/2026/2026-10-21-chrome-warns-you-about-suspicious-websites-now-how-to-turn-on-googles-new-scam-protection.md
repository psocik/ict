## Chrome Warns You About Suspicious Websites Now 🚨

Google has beefed up the security in Chrome to further protect you from becoming a victim of online scams. First up is protection against suspicious websites, and Chrome will now notify you if a site is deemed suspicious. The warning that appears on the screen may specifically tell you that the site doesn't seem right, as it's similar to other sites associated with phishing, malware, and scams.

Google rates a website as suspicious by comparing it with other deceptive or misleading sites. Gemini also plays a role here, as the AI analyzes the site's content, the brand it represents, the site's intent, the domain's reputation, and other factors to derive a scam likelihood score. With this update, Chrome can now distinguish between suspicious sites and dangerous sites by flashing different warnings. The alert for a dangerous site paints the screen red and strongly recommends that you leave the page, while the alert for a suspicious site is more subtle. **"Suspicious site warnings notify users when a page exhibits deceptive patterns commonly associated with known malicious sites,"** Google said in its blog post.

Additionally, Google has enhanced Chrome's overall scam-detection capabilities. This same type of AI-powered security will now alert you against a wider range of online scams, including fake antivirus and phony browser updates. For this, Google uses a server-side Gemini model with more advanced capabilities. The model analyzes the content of the page, the reputation of the domain, and other signals to reach a final conclusion.

Furthermore, Chrome now offers defenses against ClickFix attacks. ClickFix is a type of social engineering scam that tricks you into manually copying and running malicious commands on your own computer, ostensibly to fix a technical issue that doesn't exist. Because you carry out the actual steps yourself, existing security defenses in Chrome and other products won't catch or block the attack, at least until now. With the latest update, Chrome will now detect if you're tricked into copying suspicious commands. After you copy them but before you have a chance to paste them, the clipboard is wiped of any personal data. Chrome then analyzes that data and displays a warning if the activity is deemed dangerous.

To take advantage of these new security defenses in Chrome, you need to be running the browser in **Enhanced Protection mode**. This is the strongest level available in the program's Safe Browsing process. To enable this in Chrome on the desktop or mobile, head to Settings and select Privacy and security. On the desktop, click the option for Security; on mobile, tap the option for Safe Browsing. Then choose the setting for Enhanced Protection. You should also turn on Enhanced protection for your Google account. For this, sign in to your Google account page on the web. Select the option for Security & sign-in. Scroll down the page and select the option for Enhanced Safe Browsing for your account. Then just turn on the switch.

**"As scammers find new ways to take advantage of people, Chrome and Safe Browsing will continue to develop smarter, real-time defenses to make the web safer for everyone,"** Google said in its post. **"To make sure you're the first to get the latest protections against phishing, scams, social engineering, and malware, enable Enhanced Protection on Chrome and your Google Account to help you stay one step ahead of attackers."**
