## The Operational Value of Adversary Attribution Intelligence

This study evaluates whether attribution intelligence improves the speed and accuracy of incident response when defending against both static and adaptive adversaries. The findings of this study demonstrate that attribution intelligence reduces both detection and response times by providing defenders with actionable insight into attacker objectives, expected behaviors, and likely attack paths. **Adversary attribution intelligence**, as defined in the report, provides context about an attacker's objectives, motivations, and their tactics, techniques, and procedures (TTPs). While attribution has long been used to support cyber defense operations, its effectiveness in an environment where artificial intelligence is becoming increasingly accessible to threat actors warranted further research.

To conduct this evaluation, a controlled cyber range was developed, consisting of an Active Directory enterprise environment, Wazuh for detection and response, MITRE Caldera for adversary emulation, and PentestGPT for adaptive attack execution. Furthermore, attribution intelligence derived from publicly available threat intelligence sources was compiled into a structured profile of APT29 and used to provide contextual information during simulated attack scenarios.

The results showed that in testing Wazuh against the MITRE Caldera adversary emulation platform, enabling threat intelligence reduced detection time from 46.5 seconds to 35.6 seconds and reduced response time from 16 minutes and 45 seconds to 2 minutes and 14 seconds. These results represent an approximately 23% reduction in detection time and an approximately 87% reduction in response time, demonstrating that threat intelligence can substantially improve the speed and effectiveness of defensive investigation and response.
